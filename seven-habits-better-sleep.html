7 Everyday Habits That Can Help You Sleep Better

A practical guide from The Daily Compass

Sleep is an important part of everyday wellbeing. It gives the body and mind time to rest and recover, and getting enough quality sleep can support your mood, concentration and general health.

However, busy schedules, stress, screen time and changing routines can make it difficult to get a good night's rest. The good news is that small changes to your daily habits may help you build a healthier sleep routine.

Here are seven practical habits to consider.

1. Keep a consistent sleep schedule

Try to go to bed and wake up at roughly the same time every day, including weekends. A regular schedule can help your body develop a more predictable sleep routine.

You do not have to make a sudden or dramatic change. Start by choosing a realistic bedtime and wake-up time that fit your daily responsibilities, then try to maintain them.

2. Create a relaxing bedtime routine

Moving directly from a busy day into bed can make it harder to settle down. Give yourself some time to slow down before sleeping.

You could read a book, listen to calm music, take a warm bath, practise gentle breathing or prepare for the following day. Choose activities that help you feel relaxed rather than more alert.

3. Reduce screen time before bed

Phones, tablets, televisions and computers can keep you mentally engaged when you are trying to wind down. Notifications, social media and exciting videos may also encourage you to stay awake longer than planned.

Try putting your devices away at least 30 minutes before bedtime. You can also silence unnecessary notifications or keep your phone away from your sleeping area if practical.

4. Pay attention to caffeine and evening meals

Caffeine is found in coffee, tea, some soft drinks, energy drinks and chocolate. Because it can remain in your system for several hours, consuming it late in the day may make it harder to fall asleep.

Consider reducing afternoon and evening caffeine if you notice that it affects your sleep. Also avoid eating very large meals close to bedtime if they leave you feeling uncomfortable.

5. Make your sleeping environment comfortable

Your sleeping space can influence how easily you rest. Where possible, aim for a room that is quiet, dark and comfortably cool.

Simple changes may help, such as using curtains to reduce outside light, lowering unnecessary noise, choosing comfortable bedding and keeping the room at a temperature that suits you.

6. Stay physically active during the day

Regular physical activity can support general health and may also help improve sleep. You do not necessarily need an expensive gym membership or an intense exercise routine to get started.

Walking, cycling, dancing, household activities and other forms of movement can all contribute to an active day. Choose activities that are suitable for your abilities and circumstances.

If exercising close to bedtime makes you feel more alert, try moving your activity to an earlier part of the day.

7. Get natural daylight during the day

Spending time outdoors during daylight hours can help support your body's natural sleep-wake rhythm. Morning daylight may be particularly helpful for establishing a regular daily routine.

Consider opening your curtains in the morning, spending time outside or taking a short daytime walk when possible.

How much sleep do adults need?

Sleep needs vary with age and individual circumstances. According to the US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, most adults aged 18 to 60 need at least seven hours of sleep each night. Older adults may have different recommended ranges.

Rather than focusing only on a number, pay attention to whether you regularly wake feeling rested and can function comfortably during the day.

When to seek professional advice

Occasional difficulty sleeping happens to many people. However, if you regularly struggle to fall asleep, wake frequently during the night, feel excessively tired during the day or experience ongoing sleep difficulties, consider speaking with a qualified healthcare professional.

Sleep problems can have different causes, and professional advice can help identify what may be affecting your rest.

Final thoughts

Building a healthier sleep routine does not require changing everything at once. Start with one or two habits that feel manageable, such as keeping a consistent bedtime or reducing screen time before sleeping.

Give yourself time to see what works. Your schedule, environment and personal needs may be different from someone else's, so focus on creating a routine that fits your life.

Better rest can begin with small, thoughtful everyday choices.
