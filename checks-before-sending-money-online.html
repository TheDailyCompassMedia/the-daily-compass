Before You Send Money: 7 Checks to Make Before Paying Online

Online payments make shopping, paying bills, and sending money more convenient. But one rushed transaction can lead to financial loss. Taking a few moments to check the details before paying can help you avoid common mistakes and scams.

Whether you are paying a business, purchasing something online, or transferring money to another person, these seven checks can help you make safer payment decisions.

1. Confirm who you are paying

Before sending money, make sure you know who will receive it. Check the person's name, business identity, or account details. If you are buying from an online store, look for reliable contact information and a consistent business presence.

Be cautious if a seller uses a different name from the business you contacted or gives you unexplained reasons to pay an unfamiliar account.

2. Double-check the payment details

A small typing mistake can send money to the wrong recipient. Carefully review the account number, bank name, payment address, phone number, or other recipient information before confirming.

Where your payment service displays the recipient's name, compare it with the details provided by the person or business you intend to pay.

3. Check the website or payment page

If you are shopping online, make sure you are using the genuine website or official payment channel. Scammers sometimes create websites and messages that imitate trusted businesses.

Check the spelling of the website address. Be careful with unexpected payment links sent through text messages, emails, or social media.

4. Be careful with pressure and urgent requests

A seller or caller who insists that you must pay immediately may be trying to stop you from thinking carefully. Messages claiming that an offer will disappear in minutes, or that an account will be blocked unless you pay immediately, deserve extra attention.

Pause and verify the request independently. Contact the organisation through a phone number or website you already know to be genuine.

5. Understand the full cost before paying

Check the final amount before confirming a transaction. Depending on the service and country, the total may include delivery fees, taxes, currency conversion charges, or transaction fees.

Make sure you understand the amount that will leave your account and whether the payment is one-time or recurring.

6. Use a secure payment method

Choose a payment method from a trusted financial institution or recognised payment provider. Understand the protections and dispute process offered by that provider before using it.

Never share your password, PIN, one-time verification code, or other confidential security information with someone claiming to help you complete a payment.

7. Keep your payment confirmation

After making a payment, save the receipt, transaction reference, confirmation email, or other available proof of payment. Keep a record of the seller's details and any agreement about delivery or refunds.

If something goes wrong, contact your bank or payment provider promptly using its official contact channel. Explain what happened and ask whether the transaction can be stopped, disputed, or investigated.

What if you think you have been scammed?

Act quickly. Contact your bank or payment provider using its official website, app, or phone number. Explain the situation and follow its instructions.

Change any passwords that may have been exposed, secure affected accounts, and report the incident to the relevant consumer protection or law enforcement authority in your country where appropriate.

A simple payment checklist

  • Have I verified the person or business receiving the money?
  • Are the account and payment details correct?
  • Am I using the genuine website or payment channel?
  • Am I being pressured to pay too quickly?
  • Do I understand the full amount and any extra charges?
  • Is the payment method trustworthy?
  • Can I keep proof of this transaction?

Conclusion

Paying online should be convenient, not careless. Taking a moment to verify the recipient, review the payment details, understand the cost, and keep a receipt can help reduce avoidable risks.

Before your next online payment, slow down and check the details. A little caution can make a meaningful difference.
