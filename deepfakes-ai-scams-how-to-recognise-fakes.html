Deepfakes and AI Scams: How to Recognise What Is Real Online

A practical guide from The Daily Compass

Imagine receiving a video call from someone you know. Their face looks familiar, their voice sounds natural, and they tell you they urgently need money.

Would you immediately believe them?

Artificial intelligence is changing how people create and share digital content. Today, technology can generate realistic images, imitate voices and manipulate videos in ways that can be difficult to recognise.

While these developments have useful applications, criminals are also using them to make online scams more convincing. These manipulated images, videos and audio recordings are commonly known as deepfakes.

Understanding how they work and learning how to verify suspicious content can help protect your money, personal information and online identity.

1. What Exactly Are Deepfakes?

A deepfake is digital content that has been created or manipulated using artificial intelligence to make something appear real when it may not be.

Deepfakes can involve:

  • Fake videos: A person's face or movements are altered to make them appear to say or do something they never did.
  • Cloned voices: AI recreates someone's voice and uses it to produce new speech.
  • AI-generated images: Realistic pictures are created of people, places or situations that do not exist.
  • Manipulated recordings: Existing audio or video is changed to misrepresent what happened.

For example, a scammer could create a video that appears to show a well-known businessperson promoting an investment opportunity that the person has never endorsed.

The danger is that viewers may trust the content simply because it looks authentic.

2. How Scammers Use Deepfakes to Target People

AI-powered scams can take different forms. Here are some examples worth understanding.

Fake Celebrity and Investment Promotions

You might come across a video of a famous person recommending an investment platform and promising unusually high returns.

The video may look convincing, but the person might never have made that recommendation.

Scammers can use manipulated videos, fake testimonials and impersonated social media accounts to attract victims into fraudulent investment schemes.

Always investigate investment opportunities independently rather than relying on celebrity endorsements.

Family Emergency Voice Scams

Imagine receiving a phone call from someone who sounds exactly like your child, sibling or close friend. They claim to be in trouble and urgently need money.

A scammer may use AI voice-cloning technology to imitate someone using audio recordings available online.

Because the voice sounds familiar, you might feel pressured to act without checking the story.

Before sending money, end the call and contact the person directly using their usual phone number.

Fake Business and Workplace Requests

Scammers may impersonate business owners, managers or colleagues using cloned voices and manipulated videos.

They might request urgent payments, confidential information or access to company accounts.

An unusual financial request should always be verified through an independent communication channel, even if it appears to come from someone you recognise.

Fake Online Profiles

AI-generated photographs can make fraudulent social media accounts appear genuine.

Someone might use an artificial profile picture, a convincing personal story and natural-sounding messages to build trust before requesting money or personal information.

Be careful when interacting with unfamiliar people online, especially when conversations quickly become financial.

3. Seven Warning Signs That Something May Be Fake

Deepfakes are becoming more sophisticated, so there is no single visual clue that can reliably identify every fake. However, these warning signs can help you recognise suspicious content.

1. Unusual Facial Movements

Look for facial expressions that do not seem to match the conversation. In some manipulated videos, movements may appear unnatural or inconsistent.

However, modern deepfakes can look remarkably realistic, so this clue alone is not enough.

2. Audio and Lip-Sync Problems

Watch whether the person's mouth movements match the words you hear. A noticeable delay or mismatch may indicate manipulation.

3. Strange Lighting or Shadows

Pay attention to inconsistent lighting, unusual shadows or visual distortions around the face and body. These can sometimes appear in lower-quality manipulated videos.

4. Unexpected Requests for Money

If someone suddenly requests money, bank information or a security code, especially while creating a sense of panic, stop and verify their identity.

5. Unbelievable Investment Promises

Be cautious of advertisements promising guaranteed profits, effortless wealth or unusually high returns. A familiar face in an advertisement does not prove that the investment is legitimate.

6. Unusual Communication Behaviour

A familiar person might suddenly contact you from a different account, demand secrecy or insist that you must act immediately. These changes deserve careful attention.

7. No Reliable Source for the Content

If a dramatic video or photograph is circulating online, investigate where it originated.

Check whether credible news organisations, official websites or verified accounts have independently confirmed the information.

4. Eight Practical Ways to Protect Yourself

You do not need to be a technology expert to protect yourself from AI-powered scams. These simple habits can make a meaningful difference.

1. Verify Unexpected Calls

If someone calls claiming to be a family member or friend and requests urgent financial assistance, end the call and contact them through a number you already know.

2. Create a Family Safety Code

Agree on a private word or phrase with close family members. If someone contacts you claiming to be in an emergency, ask for the agreed code.

This provides another way to check identity when a voice may have been cloned.

3. Never Share Verification Codes

Do not give anyone your banking PIN, password, one-time password or account recovery code.

Be especially cautious of unexpected calls or messages asking you to reveal sensitive security information.

4. Check Information From Multiple Sources

Before believing or sharing a shocking video, look for confirmation from reliable independent sources. A single social media post is not enough evidence that something happened.

5. Protect Your Personal Content

Be thoughtful about the photographs, videos and voice recordings you share publicly.

Review your social media privacy settings and avoid giving unfamiliar people unnecessary access to personal information.

6. Investigate Investment Opportunities

Before transferring money, research the company, check its official website and investigate whether it is properly authorised in your country.

Never invest simply because a familiar person appears to recommend an opportunity in a video.

7. Pause Before Acting

Scammers often rely on fear, excitement and urgency. If someone insists that you must send money immediately, take a moment to think and verify the request.

8. Report Suspicious Content

If you encounter a suspected deepfake scam, report the account or content to the platform where you found it.

If you lose money, contact your bank or payment provider immediately and report the incident to the appropriate authorities in your country.

5. Can AI Detection Tools Tell You If a Video Is Fake?

Some online tools claim to identify AI-generated images, videos and audio recordings.

They may examine visual inconsistencies, audio patterns or information embedded in a digital file.

However, these tools are not perfect. A detection tool may incorrectly classify genuine content or fail to recognise a sophisticated deepfake.

Some social media platforms also compress or modify uploaded media, which can make analysis more difficult.

Use detection tools as an additional source of information, not as final proof.

6. Are Deepfakes Always Dangerous?

Not necessarily. Deepfake technology can also have legitimate applications.

For example, it can be used in film production, visual effects, entertainment, educational demonstrations and creative projects.

The problem arises when manipulated content is presented as genuine to deceive, exploit or harm someone.

The real concern is how the technology is used and whether viewers are given enough information to understand what they are seeing.

7. What Should You Do If You Have Been Targeted?

If you believe you have encountered a deepfake scam, take action quickly.

  • Stop communicating with the suspected scammer.
  • Do not send additional money or personal information.
  • Contact your bank or payment provider immediately if you made a payment.
  • Change compromised passwords and secure affected accounts.
  • Report the suspicious account or content to the relevant platform.
  • Preserve messages, phone numbers, transaction records and other evidence.
  • Report the incident to the appropriate authorities in your country.

Conclusion

Artificial intelligence is changing how people communicate, work and interact online. It is also creating new opportunities for criminals to impersonate others and manipulate digital content.

As deepfake technology improves, relying only on what we see or hear may no longer be enough.

The most important protection is to develop a habit of checking before trusting, sharing or sending money.

Verify unexpected requests, question suspicious investment promotions and protect your personal information.

You do not have to understand every detail of artificial intelligence to stay safer online. You simply need to know when to pause, investigate and verify.
