Passkeys Explained: How to Sign In Without a Password

A practical guide from The Daily Compass

Passwords are part of everyday life. We use them to access email, social media, shopping accounts, banking services and many other online platforms. But remembering multiple passwords can be difficult, and poorly protected passwords can put accounts at risk.

A newer sign-in method called a passkey offers another option. Instead of typing a password, you can approve a sign-in using your phone, computer or another trusted device.

Here is how passkeys work, why they are useful and how you can get started.

What Exactly Is a Passkey?

A passkey is a digital sign-in credential designed to replace traditional passwords.

It uses a security technology called public-key cryptography. When you create a passkey, your device generates a pair of related digital keys. The website or app receives the public key, while the private key remains protected by your device or credential manager.

When you want to sign in, your device uses the passkey to confirm your identity. You may be asked to use your fingerprint, face recognition, device PIN or screen lock.

Unlike a traditional password, you do not have to remember or type the passkey yourself.

Why Are Passkeys Becoming Popular?

1. They Help Protect Against Phishing

Phishing happens when criminals use fake websites, emails or messages to trick people into revealing sensitive information.

Passkeys are connected to the legitimate website or app for which they were created. This makes them much harder to use on fake lookalike websites than ordinary passwords.

2. You Do Not Have to Remember Another Password

You approve the sign-in using your device's existing security method, such as a PIN or fingerprint.

3. They Are Unique to Each Service

A passkey is created for a particular account and service. It is not a password that you copy and paste across multiple websites.

4. They Can Make Signing In Simpler

On supported devices and services, you may be able to sign in with a quick biometric or screen-lock confirmation instead of entering a username and password.

How to Set Up a Passkey

The exact steps depend on the website, device and password manager you use. However, the general process is straightforward.

Step 1: Choose an Account That Supports Passkeys

Start with an important account, such as your email or another service that offers passkey sign-in.

Step 2: Open the Official Website or App

Go directly to the service's official website or open its genuine app. Avoid following unexpected links in messages claiming to help you create a passkey.

Step 3: Find the Security Settings

Look for a section called Security, Sign-in options, Passkeys or something similar.

Step 4: Select the Passkey Option

If the service supports it, choose the option to create or add a passkey.

Step 5: Follow Your Device's Instructions

Your phone or computer may ask you to confirm using a fingerprint, face recognition, PIN or screen lock.

Step 6: Check Where Your Passkey Is Stored

Some passkeys can synchronise across devices through a supported credential manager. Others may be tied to a particular device or security key.

Check the available options and understand how you can recover access if your device is lost or replaced.

Are Passkeys Completely Risk-Free?

No online security method removes every risk.

Your device still needs to be protected. Use a screen lock, keep its software updated and secure the account that manages your saved passkeys.

It is also important to understand how you would recover access if your phone or computer were lost, damaged or replaced. Where available, consider adding another trusted device or security key and reviewing the service's account-recovery options.

Not every website supports passkeys yet, and the experience may differ between devices and services.

Should You Start Using Passkeys?

If a trusted service offers passkeys, they can be a useful alternative to traditional passwords. You do not need to change every account at once.

Start with one important account, follow the official setup instructions and make sure you understand its recovery options.

Final Thoughts

Passkeys are a practical development in online security. They can make signing in easier while reducing some of the risks associated with passwords and phishing.

As more services adopt the technology, understanding how it works can help you make safer choices online.
